GEO for B2B SaaS: winning the comparison question.

Industry24 February 20267 min readBy Isolation Media

The B2B software buying journey used to start with a review site or a colleague's recommendation. Increasingly it starts with a question to an assistant: what are the best tools for this job, at our size, in our industry. The answer names three products, and everything downstream, the demos, the trials, the deal, flows from that shortlist.

If you are not in the three, you are not in the process, and you will never see the loss in any dashboard.

The comparison question is the whole game

SaaS buyers ask comparatively by nature. Which is better for us, how does X compare to Y, what should we use instead of Z. These questions do more to determine pipeline than any category level query, and they are decided by evidence you may not control.

The sources engines lean on for comparison are review platforms, independent comparison content, community discussion and, when it exists, credible first party comparison from the vendors themselves. Most vendors either avoid comparison content entirely or produce competitor pages so obviously biased that they read as marketing and get discounted.

How to write comparison content engines will actually use

The rule is simple and most companies cannot bring themselves to follow it: be genuinely fair. State clearly where the competitor is the better choice.

A comparison page that concludes you win on every dimension is worthless as evidence. A page that says you are the better fit for teams of a certain size in a certain situation, and that the alternative is better for a different situation, is credible, quotable and does not lose you deals you were going to win anyway. It loses you deals you were going to lose slowly and expensively.

  • Name the competitor plainly rather than alluding to them
  • Compare on the dimensions buyers actually weigh, including price
  • State who each product suits, honestly
  • Keep it current, because stale comparisons are worse than none

Category definition is a strategic lever

Engines answer within categories. If your product sits awkwardly between two established categories, buyers asking about either may never see you. You have two options: fit yourself clearly into an existing category buyers already ask about, or invest heavily in making your own category legible enough that people ask about it. The second is expensive and occasionally correct. The first is usually right.

A comparison page where you win everything is marketing. One where you sometimes lose is evidence.

Where reviews fit

Software review platforms are heavily weighted by engines because they are structured, plentiful and independent. Depth of recent reviews mentioning specific use cases and company sizes matters more than a marginal difference in average score, because specifics let an engine match you to a situation.

Documentation is underrated evidence

Public, well written product documentation is quietly one of the strongest GEO assets a SaaS company has. It is detailed, factual, technical and unambiguous, which is exactly what a model wants to cite when someone asks whether your product can do a specific thing. Gating it behind a login removes it from the evidence base entirely.

Our audit maps the comparison questions in your category and shows which products get named for each. For most vendors the surprise is not who wins, but how often the answer comes from a source they have never thought about.
