GEO vs SEO: what carries over and what doesn't.

Strategy9 June 20266 min readBy Isolation Media

The most common question we get from owners who built real organic traffic over the years: is all that work now worthless? The honest answer is no, but it is no longer sufficient, and a few of the habits that built it will actively hold you back if you keep them.

What transfers directly

The foundations of good SEO are the foundations of GEO too, and if you have them, you start ahead.

  • A crawlable, fast, well structured site. Retrieval based engines have to read your pages before they can quote them. Everything technical that helped Google helps them.
  • Genuine authority. Real reviews, earned media and a consistent reputation were the slow, unglamorous part of SEO. They are the core of GEO.
  • Content that actually answers things. If your pages were written for readers rather than crawlers, they are already close to quotable.

Where the disciplines split

The differences are less about tactics and more about what you are optimising for.

The unit of competition changed. SEO competed for positions on a page the user could see. GEO competes for inclusion in an answer the user cannot see being formed. There is no position four. You are named or you are absent.

The surface area changed. SEO focused overwhelmingly on your own site. In GEO, what the rest of the web says about you often outweighs what you say about yourself, because engines treat independent sources as evidence and your site as testimony.

The click stopped being the point. A growing share of answers end without any click at all. The recommendation itself is the value, which is why measuring visits alone now undercounts what visibility earns you.

What to stop doing

A few inherited habits now cost more than they return.

  • Volume publishing. Fifty thin posts a month made sense when every page was a lottery ticket. To an engine weighing evidence, a large body of shallow content dilutes your entity rather than strengthening it.
  • Keyword contortions. Pages twisted around exact match phrases read badly to humans and models alike. Engines answer questions, so write to questions.
  • Chasing every topic. Breadth worked when each ranking was independent. Consensus does not form around a generalist. Pick the questions you deserve to win and go deep.
SEO optimised what your site said. GEO optimises what the web agrees about you.

The practical takeaway

Keep the foundations, keep the honest content, and redirect the effort that went into rankings toward evidence: original data, real reviews, presence in the sources your engines cite. Do not run the two disciplines as rivals. Run them as one programme with a new definition of winning.
