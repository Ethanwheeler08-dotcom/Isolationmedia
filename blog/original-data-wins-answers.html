Why original data is the fastest way into AI answers.

Strategy18 May 20266 min readBy Isolation Media

Here is a pattern we see constantly in answer tracking: a mid sized company publishes one genuinely original piece of research, and within months it is being cited by engines in answers its far larger competitors used to own. Not because the company is famous. Because it produced the only real number in the room.

Why engines love data

An assistant composing an answer is hunting for things it can state with confidence. Opinions are everywhere and contradict each other. A specific figure from a named source is different: it is concrete, attributable and useful. When the only available answer to "what does X typically cost" or "how long does Y actually take" comes from your survey of your own market, the engine has one place to go, and it goes there repeatedly.

Better still, other publications cite data too. One good study earns mentions in trade press and newsletters, and each mention is another independent source associating your name with your subject. The engine sees consensus forming, and consensus is what gets you named.

You have more data than you think

Owners hear "original research" and imagine commissioning a polling firm. Usually the material is already sitting in the business.

  • Operational numbers. Real timelines, real cost ranges, real failure rates across hundreds of jobs. Anonymised and aggregated, this is the exact information buyers ask assistants for and almost nobody publishes.
  • A simple survey of your customers or industry. Fifty thoughtful responses to well designed questions beat a thousand responses to lazy ones.
  • Benchmarks from your own tools. If your product or process generates measurements, an annual report built on them can become the reference for your category.

Publishing it so it gets cited

The packaging decides whether the work pays. A few rules we follow on every study.

  • Lead with the finding. The headline number in the first paragraph, stated plainly, so it can be quoted without excavation.
  • Show the method. Sample size, time period, how it was gathered. Engines and journalists both check whether a number looks defensible.
  • Keep a stable home. One permanent page per study, updated annually, rather than a scatter of posts. Citations accumulate to an address.
  • Give it away. No gate, no email wall. A gated study cannot be read by the things you want quoting it.
In a war of opinions, the only measurement wins.

One caution: the data must be real, and the method must survive scrutiny. An invented statistic that gets cited widely is a reputational bomb with your name printed on it. The whole point is to be the source engines can trust. Earn it once and it keeps paying for years.
