GEO for ecommerce: getting your products into answers.

Industry14 April 20267 min readBy Isolation Media

Ecommerce spent fifteen years optimising for a shopper who browses. That shopper is being replaced by one who asks: what is the best option for my situation, and why. The answer they get names two or three products, and the rest of the market is not in the conversation.

What changes about product content

Product pages were written to persuade someone already looking at them. Now they need to inform a model deciding whether to mention you at all, which rewards different writing.

  • Specifications belong in text. Dimensions, materials, compatibility, capacity, care requirements. If it is only in an image or a spec sheet graphic, it does not exist to an engine.
  • Say who it is not for. Counterintuitive and highly effective. Stating the limits of a product lets a model recommend it confidently for the right situation, and confidence is what gets you named.
  • Answer the comparison question. Buyers ask how your product compares to alternatives. If you never address it, the engine uses a competitor's or a review site's framing instead of yours.
  • Use case over feature list. People ask by situation, not by specification. Content organised around situations matches more questions.

Reviews do double duty

Product reviews are simultaneously your conversion tool and your primary GEO evidence. Engines read them heavily for exactly the details product copy avoids: how it holds up over time, what surprised people, who it did not suit.

Which means review volume alone is not the goal. Reviews that mention specific use cases are worth more than generic praise, and prompting for specifics in your post purchase request measurably changes what engines can extract.

Category pages become guides

A traditional category page is a grid with a paragraph of text underneath. To an engine it is nearly content free. The version that earns citations is a genuine buying guide: what the options are, how to choose between them, what matters at different budgets, what the common mistakes are.

This feels like a lot of work for a page that used to be a filter interface. It is also the page most likely to be quoted when someone asks how to choose in your category.

The marketplace tension

If you sell through marketplaces as well as your own site, engines frequently cite the marketplace listing rather than you, because it carries more reviews and more traffic. You cannot fully prevent this, but you can make your own pages the better source by putting information there that the marketplace listing cannot hold: depth, comparison, guides, genuine expertise.

The shopper who browses is being replaced by one who asks. Category pages built for browsing have nothing to say to them.

Structured data is not optional here

Product schema with accurate price, availability and rating is the baseline. It is how an engine states a fact about your product without hedging, and in ecommerce the hedge usually costs you the mention.
