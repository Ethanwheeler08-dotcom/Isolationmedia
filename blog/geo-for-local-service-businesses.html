GEO for local service businesses.

Industry26 May 20266 min readBy Isolation Media

Local service businesses should be winning AI answers. The questions are simple, the buying decision is fast, the customer usually has no existing provider, and the field in any given suburb is small. Instead most are invisible, and the reason is almost always the same handful of mistakes.

The urgency advantage

A large share of local service demand is urgent: a blocked drain, a broken air conditioner in February, a locked door at midnight. Urgent buyers do the least research and accept the first credible recommendation. There is no channel where being the named answer converts better.

Urgent questions also have unusually clear shapes. Someone asking for an emergency plumber in a named suburb at 11pm is asking a question with a right answer, and an engine will give one if it can find a business that has stated availability, service area and response time plainly.

The suburb page mistake

Nearly every local business has been sold the same tactic: generate a page for every suburb, swap the name, publish two hundred of them. It worked adequately in old search. It actively hurts you now.

Engines treat near duplicate pages as low quality, and a site made mostly of them looks like filler. Worse, the pages say nothing an engine can use, because a suburb name inserted into boilerplate contains no information about whether you actually serve that suburb well.

The replacement is fewer, substantially different pages for the areas that genuinely matter to your business, each containing real specifics: response times to that area, jobs you commonly do there, the quirks of the local housing stock, any surcharge or travel consideration. Ten real pages outperform two hundred generated ones.

What to publish instead

  • Straight answers to your five most common phone questions. Whatever people ask before booking, answered in plain text, including the awkward ones about price.
  • Price guidance. Ranges, call out fees, what changes the number. Most competitors refuse to publish this, which makes the business that does the one an engine can answer with.
  • Availability and response reality. What after hours actually means, how fast you get to each area, what the wait is in peak season.
  • The problem content. How to tell whether a thing needs repair or replacement, what a noise means, what to do before the technician arrives. These get quoted constantly and they demonstrate competence.
Two hundred suburb pages tell an engine nothing. One honest pricing page tells it everything.

Reviews are the whole ballgame

In local services, review consensus outweighs everything else. A business with two hundred recent, detailed reviews will be recommended over a business with better content and twelve reviews, nearly every time. If you do nothing else, build a reliable habit of asking every satisfied customer, on the platform your category actually uses.
